LLMs earn their place by proposing a different way to tell the scene and explaining its cost. Moving timestamps and selecting a predeclared lav alternative do not demonstrate that. Today Supervisor prose goes into the AD prompt; there is no subsequent candidate critique (`coordinator/loop.py:258`). The deterministic checker cannot establish dramatic confidentiality; the UI correctly calls `must_keep` human-reviewed prose (`coordinator/loop.py:304`).

A skeptical Switch engineer asks: “Did two real agents collaborate, or did a Python harness send predetermined JSON?” W6 proves the transport and coordinator, not model reasoning.

The previews also overpromise if called storyboards: blocking/camera positions are location presets, with shot count driving extra marks (`ui/src/sets/maquette.ts:31`). Call them set models and show the meaningful coverage difference.

Most seriously, “affected” currently means **earliest call changed** (`coordinator/release.py:63`). Rooftop relocation and recorder replacement require zero fresh acknowledgments in W6. Yet `ui/src/story.ts:114` says “Everyone is on release.” People can have unchanged calls and obsolete instructions. This contradicts the product promise while complying with W6’s narrower contract.
